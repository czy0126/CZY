Abstract-Datacenter usage tends to increase as artificial 
intelligence (AI) grows and becomes more prevalent. High-
power density and high-efficiency power converters are 
required for datacenter applications. This paper presents a 48V-
2.2V matrix autotransformer switched-capacitor DC-DC 
converter (MASC) for data center applications. The high 
voltage side of MASC takes advantage of switched tank 
converter. The low voltage rectifier side of the MASC leveraged 
a new magnetic integrated autotransformer. Compared with a 
switched tank converter, MASC needs fewer switches on the 
low-voltage rectifier side to achieve the same conversion ratio. 
Compared to LLC converters using isolation transformers, the 
use of matrix autotransformers reduces the power loss and the 
size of the magnetic core. Simulation results are provided to 
verify the feasibility. A 48V-2.2V, 96.1% peak efficiency 22X-
MASC has been built and tested.

converter, 
DC-DC, 
datacenter, autotransformer. 
I. INTRODUCTION  
 
The growth of AI is a significant driver of increased 
datacenter usage, leading to the development of more 
powerful and efficient datacenter infrastructure to meet the 
rising demand. High efficiency and power density power 
converter is required for CPU power supply [1]-[6]. Fig. 1 
shows the power train of datacenter application. In the power 
train of the datacenter application, the input voltage of is 
normally 480V AC voltage. Firstly, it is converted to a 400-V 
DC bus. Then the 400V DC is converted to 48V, which finally 
will be converted to 1V to the CPU/GPUs. To increase 
efficiency. The 48V DC firstly be converted to an intermediate 
voltage such as 12V, 6V, 3.3V, etc. Then there is a buck stage 
converter to the voltage CPU/GPU needs. The stage called 
Intermediate Bus Converter (IBC). There are also solutions 
that convert 48V directly to 1V [7]-[13]. Datacenter 
applications characteristic low-voltage and high-current 
properties. Switched-capacitor DC–DC converters have 
gained popularity in data center applications due to their high-
efficiency, high power density performance in such 
application scenario [14]-[16]. A Family of Zero Current 
Switching Multilevel Modular Switched Capacitor Converter 
(ZCS-MMCCC) is modulable and achieves different 
conversion ratios [17]. Switched-Tank Converter (STC) is 
proposed through removing the non-resonant capacitors and 
inserting resonant inductors in ZCS-MMCCC. Switch-tank 
converter has a peak efficiency of 98.55% with 54V input and 
9V output, 750W/in3 [18]. Through using stray inductance in 
the circuit, it can achieve Zero Current Switching (ZCS). 
Through implementing a phase shifting control method, the 
RMS current and is reduced and Zero Voltage Switching 
(ZVS) is achieved. However, it can’t achieve ZCS and ZVS at 
the same time. Apart from that, a lower CPU voltage input is 
needed to realize heavy computation tasks since early 2000. 
Lower intermediate DC bus voltage is needed to lower 
conversion ratio of second stage and improve the whole 
system efficiency.

With the conversion ratio increase, the conduction loss of it 
will decrease the efficiency greatly. And the size will increase 
greatly with the conversion ratio increase, which will decrease 
the power density. It is challenging to keep the efficiency 
relatively high with the increased conversion ratio. Since the 
number of MOSFET used in traditional STC converter has a 
linear relationship with the conversion ratio. The main 
contribution to the power loss increases when the converter 
ratio increases for STC is the conduction loss. Apart from 
Switched-capacitor converter, LLC converter can also achieve 
relatively high conversion ratio in datacenter application by 
using transformer. Various kinds of autotransformers-based 
LLC as IBCs are proposed [19]-[22]. Besides, the current 
doubler rectifier can better leverage the low volage 
semiconductor devices. Although LLC can realize high 
conversion ratio, the transformer will make the whole size 
relatively high. And in data center applications, the isolation 
feature is not needed. With all the consideration, there are two 
trends of IBCs for datacenter application. The first trend is to 
merge the IBC stage and the buck stage together: The first 
stage is switched-capacitor converter. It doesn’t have any 
magnetic components. The second stage is regulated buck 
converter or multiphase series buck converter. The second 
trend is to combine the advantage of STC and LLC together. 
It adopts autotransformer to replace the isolated transformer 
in traditional LLC to realize high conversion ratio IBC. 
 
This paper presents a 48V-2.2V (22X) Matrix 
Autotransformer Switched Capacitor DC-DC Converter 
(22X-MASC) topology for 48V to 2.2V application. The high 
voltage side circuit of 22X-MASC is the same as the STC. The 
rectifier side of the 22X-MASC is similar to LLC. MASC 
combines the advantage of STC and LLC at the same time. An 
autotransformer with 2:1:1:2 winding ratio is used. The use of 
an integrated matrix autotransformer makes MASC use a 
smaller number of low voltage side switches to achieve the 
same conversion ratio compared to STC. Besides, 22X-
MASC can achieve ZCS and ZVS at the same time. It uses 
magnetic inductance and resonant capacitor to achieve ZCS. 
Through using magnetic leakage inductance, ZVS can be 
achieved at the same time. These features increase the 
efficiency of 22X-MASC. Apart from that, the 2:1:1:2 
winding auto-transformer is integrated to increase the power 
density of 22X-MASC. In one word, 22X-MASC has higher 
efficiency than STC and a smaller size than LLC at the same 
conversion ratio [23]-[27]. The electical loops of the  gate 
driver of each paralle switches on the low votlage side are 
designed systematical and as short as it can be. This can allow 
the switches are turned on and off at the same time and reduce 
the EMI effect [28]. A 48V-2.2V 22X-MASC hardware 
prototype is designed and built to verify the feasibility. The 
prototype achieved a peak efficiency of 96.1%. There is 
further work that needs to be performed for the hardware 
prototype including pushing it to the maximum load and fine 
tune the hardware parameters including switching frequency 
and deadtime to maximum its efficiency. Besides, the 
calculated efficiency and power loss breakdown needs to 
further analyze for the potential power loss based on the 
experiment results right now. Along with the detail 
analyzation, high-voltage side device selection, winding PCB 
structure comparation will be included in the future journal 
paper. 
The rest of this article is organized as follows. Section II 
A introduces the proposed 22X-MASC topology. Section II B 
illustrate the working principles of 22X-MASC and the detail 
waveforms. Section II C will explain the process of the device 
selection and the design of the phase leg MOSFET numbers. 
At the same time, a power loss comparation of 22X-MASC 
and 22X-STC will be shown. The simulation result will be 
shown in Section II D. The experimental result and 
measurement results of the hardware prototype are presented 
in Section III. Finally, Section V concludes this paper and 
future working expectation will be discussed.

IV. CONCLUTION AND FUTURE WORK 
This paper presented 22X-MASC as a solution of IBC for 
data center application. It has several advantages. It combines 
the benefits of traditional STC and LLC converters. It uses 
less switches to achieve the same conversion ratio compared 
to 22X-STC. It has a higher efficiency than 22X-STC based 
on TISL results. A 48V-2.2V MASC hardware prototype is 
built and tested. The size of it is 52mm×52mm×7.8mm. The 
prototype achieved a peak efficiency of 96.1%. Due to board 
shipment delay, there are further work need to be performed 
for the hardware prototype including pushing it to the 
maximum load and fine tune the hardware parameters 
including switching frequency and deadtime to maximum its 
efficiency. Besides, the calculated efficiency and power loss 
breakdown needs to further analyze for the potential power 
loss based on the experiment results right now. Along with 
the detail analyzation, high-voltage side device selection, 
winding PCB structure comparation will be included in the 
future journal paper.
