Single-Stage 48 V/1.8 V Converter With a Novel
Integrated Magnetics and 1000 W/in3
Power Density
Xin Lou
, Graduate Student Member, IEEE, and Qiang Li
, Member, IEEE
Abstract—High-efﬁciency, high-power-density 48-V volt-
age regulator modules are crucial and also challenging for
48-V data center power architecture. In this article, a novel
single-stage 48 V/1.8 V converter for high-performance mi-
croprocessors is proposed, offering ultrahigh power den-
sity and high efﬁciency. The converter utilizes a novel
magnetic structure that integrates the transformers and
four-phase coupled inductors of two current doubler rec-
tiﬁers. The magnetic structure design guidelines highlight
the tradeoff between the coupling coefﬁcient and magnetic
core size. To minimize core and winding losses while main-
taining compactness, small core legs are employed in cor-
ner areas, replacing the bulky leakage legs. In addition,
the printed circuit board winding arrangement is optimized,
with the use of buried vias further reducing winding loss.
Finally, a 48 V/1.8 V prototype is designed with 93.1% peak
efﬁciency and 1037 W/in3 power density, including its gate
driver loss and size. The prototype can handle 120 A con-
tinuous current and 300 A peak current for 2 ms × 62 Hz,
with 500 linear feet per minute air cooling.
Index Terms—Coupled inductor, magnetic integration,
voltage regulator modules (VRMs).

II. MAGNETIC INTEGRATION OF MULTIPHASE HALF-BRIDGE
CONVERTER WITH CURRENT DOUBLER RECTIFIERS
A conventional current doubler rectiﬁer requires three mag-
netic components, which include one transformer and two out-
put inductors. However, the bulky transformer and inductors
greatly increase the footprint, and the terminations between the
transformer and the inductors will create additional termination

losses. To improve the power density and reduce the termination
loss, a method for integrating the transformer and output induc-
tors of one current doubler rectiﬁer is proposed in [25]. The
method utilizes the magnetizing inductance of a transformer
to realize the functions of an output inductor. Therefore, the
magnetic ﬂux in the magnetic core after integration is the same
as in the output inductors, which means the cross-sectional area
of the magnetic core after integration is the same as the two
output inductors and the transformer is a gift. Furthermore, it
creates a negative coupling between the two inductors of one
current doubler rectiﬁer by adding an air gap to the leakage leg
of the EI core.
As the power consumption of microprocessors continues to
increase, one half-bridge converter with a current doubler rec-
tiﬁer cannot provide the large current (>100 A) required, and
thus the multiphase half-bridge converter with current doubler
rectiﬁers is required, as shown in Fig. 1. The control signals
with phase shift are shown in Fig. 1(b) to realize multiphase
operation. The dividing capacitors C1–C4 can be designed to
meet a speciﬁc voltage ripple limit.

steady-state inductance Lss determines the inductor current rip-
ple in the steady-state operation and the leakage inductance Lk
determines the dc ﬂux in the core. As a result, with a larger

C. Optimization of the PCB Winding Structure
To provide a high output current, the secondary windings
of the proposed structure are connected in parallel. However,
because of the integrated coupled inductors, the accumulation
of magnetomotive force (MMF) leads to an unbalanced current
among paralleled secondary windings [30]. As a result, the
current sharing of the paralleled secondary windings needs to
be analyzed. The current excitations of the proposed structure
are shown in Fig. 14. Ip is the primary-side winding current, Is is
the secondary-side winding current, ITRs is the secondary-side
current of the ideal transformer, and IL is the inductor current.
The primary-side winding current is a pure ac excitation
without dc bias, but the secondary-side winding current has
dc bias because the magnetizing inductor works as the output
inductor. Using the superposition method, the secondary-side
winding current can be seen as the sum of the transformer
current and the inductor current. For the transformer, a sym-
metrical PCB layer arrangement with an alternating MMF can
achieve good current sharing between paralleled windings [7].
For the inductor, moving the windings close to each other can
improvethecurrentsharing[30].Finally,threesymmetricalPCB
arrangements in Fig. 15 are compared, where both the trans-
former and inductor are considered. “SPPS” means the windings
are arranged as “secondary winding, primary winding, primary
winding, secondary winding,” and the top four and bottom four
windings are symmetrical. The “SPPS” and “PSSP” have an
alternating MMF, so they provide good current sharing for the

The proposed converter can achieve an efﬁciency level similar
to other best practices described in the literature. Furthermore,
the power density is much higher than the other single-stage
solutions in the literature under similar working conditions.

Second, the integrated magnetics make the transformer a gift

As a comparison, in the hybrid switched-capacitor converters
presented in [12], [13], [14], [15], [16], [17], and [31], a lot of

step-down ratio, and output inductors must exist to regulate the
output voltage. As a result, the power density is lower than the
proposed solution. Furthermore, the hybrid switched-capacitor
converters in [12], [13], [14], [15], and [16] limit the switching
frequency below 350 kHz for a high efﬁciency, which will limit
the control bandwidth and cannot satisfy the requirements of
VRs. The state-of-the-art VRs require 600–800 kHz switching
frequency. In addition, a solution that integrates the transformer
and output inductor is presented in [22]. Despite its integration,
thesolutionemploysnoncoupledinductors,resultinginalimited
power density. Furthermore, the inefﬁciency of the magnetic
structure leads to an efﬁciency reduction of over 2% as compared
to the nonintegrated version, as demonstrated in [22]. Except for
them, the sigma converter in [19] has high efﬁciency and power
density. However, for wide-voltage-range applications like VRs,
the gain of the LLC resonant converter in the sigma converter
must be changed to meet the design constraints, as described in
[19]. Therefore, to satisfy the gain requirements, the maximum
output current of the sigma converter is limited. What makes
things worse is that the quasi-parallel structure of the sigma
converter brings an additional double pole, so it is difﬁcult to
achieve a high control bandwidth [20]. These shortcomings limit
the application of sigma converters, especially as VRs. On the
contrary, the proposed solution uses a well-known topology,
which is called the half-bridge converter with current doubler
rectiﬁers. Some companies, like Texas Instruments and Inﬁneon,
provide products to support this topology to work as VRs [32],
[33]. The reference design in [33] uses constant on-time cur-
rent mode control, which is called DCAP+ control by Texas
Instruments. As described in [33], a half-bridge converter with
a current doubler rectiﬁer and DCAP+ control can achieve a
superior transient response. The limitation of the controllers is
that they only support one half-bridge converter with a current
doubler rectiﬁer.
V. CONCLUSION
In this work, a novel high-current dc–dc converter was pro-
posed.Anovelintegratedmagneticsisproposedforamultiphase
half-bridge converter with current doubler rectiﬁers. The peak
efﬁciency of the prototype was 93.1%, which was similar to
other single-stage solutions in the literature. The magnetic inte-
gration was optimized to improve power density and reduce loss.
With the novel magnetic structure, the power density could be
signiﬁcantly improved to 1037 W/in3. As a result, the proposed
converter showed great potential in high-current point-of-load
(POL) applications.
